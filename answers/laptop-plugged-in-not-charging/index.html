Laptop plugged in but not charging: what it usually is

Four different parts can cause exactly this message. Two of them are cheap, one is a battery, one is a board repair. A few checks at home narrow it down before you spend anything.

Updated 2026-09-10Written from the Whitefield bench, checked against manufacturer guidance

Quick answer

If the laptop runs on the charger but the percentage never rises, the battery is probably worn or a battery-care limit is set. If it will not run on the charger at all, suspect the adapter, the port or the charging circuit, in that order. If it charges only when the plug is held at an angle, it is the jack. A new battery fixes only the first case.

Likely causes

CommonWorn battery
After a few hundred cycles the pack stops accepting a full charge. Windows shows "plugged in, not charging" at a fixed percentage or the runtime collapses. Battery replacement
CommonFaulty adapter or cable
Frayed cables and tired bricks deliver too little current. The laptop may run but never charge.
CommonWorn charging port or DC jack
Charging only at an angle, or when the cable is wedged, is the port. Port repair
SometimesBattery-care limit or driver
Many laptops cap charging at 60–80% to protect the battery. A stuck battery driver can also freeze the reading.
SometimesCharging IC on the board
The chip that manages charging has failed. The battery and adapter are fine, and only a board-level repair fixes it. Chip-level repair

Safe checks you can do first

  1. Look at the charger light. If it goes off when you plug into the laptop, there is a short on the laptop side; stop and bring it in.
  2. Try a known-good charger of the same wattage if you can borrow one. If it charges, your adapter is the fault.
  3. Check the maker's battery app or Windows settings for a charge limit (often called battery care, conservation or health mode).
  4. Feel the plug in the port: if charging returns when you angle or push it, the jack is worn.
  5. On Windows, run powercfg /batteryreport in a command prompt and compare design capacity with full-charge capacity. A big gap means a worn battery.

What not to do

  • Do not buy a battery before the charger and port are ruled out; it is the most common wasted purchase we see.
  • Do not use a lower-wattage or unbranded charger for long; it strains the charging circuit.
  • Do not keep pushing a loose plug; it cracks the solder joints around the jack.

When it is a repair, and which one

If your checks point at the battery, it is a battery replacement. If the adapter is fine and the port is solid but nothing charges, it is a charging-circuit repair on the board, which we do at component level. If it charges only at an angle, it is a port repair. We test all four in order at the bench; diagnosis is free.

Questions people ask

Before you decide

Why does my HP / Dell / Lenovo laptop say plugged in, not charging at 100%?

That is normal: the battery is full and the laptop runs from the mains to protect the cells. It becomes a fault only when the percentage is low and still not rising.

My laptop is not charging and not turning on. Is it the battery?

Probably not only the battery. A laptop with a dead battery still runs on a working charger. Not turning on at all points at the adapter, the port or the charging circuit.

Laptop not turning on: what it usually is.

Can a charging IC be repaired?

Yes. It is a component on the board and we replace it in-house. Authorised centres usually quote a whole new board for the same fault.

Why is my laptop overheating while charging?

Charging generates heat and so does use; together they overwhelm blocked cooling. If the base is uncomfortably hot, the cooling needs a service. A swollen or hot battery itself needs urgent attention.
