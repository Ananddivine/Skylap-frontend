Laptop charger not working: is it the adapter or the laptop?

A charger that has stopped working is the cheapest thing that can go wrong with a laptop, but only if it really is the charger. The same symptom can come from the port or the charging circuit inside the laptop, and buying a new adapter will not fix those.

Quick answer

If the charger light stays off on its own, unplugged from the laptop, the adapter or its cable has failed. If the light is on but goes dim or off the moment you plug into the laptop, the fault is on the laptop side, often a short, so stop plugging it in. If charging works only with the plug held at an angle, it is the laptop's port. A known-good charger of the same wattage settles it in a minute.

Likely causes

CommonFailed adapter or cable
The cable breaks inside near the plug or the brick, or the brick itself fails. The light flickers when you move the cable, or stays off. Most adapter bricks are sealed units and are replaced rather than repaired. Chargers in stock
CommonWrong or underpowered charger
A charger with lower wattage than the original, or a USB-C charger without the right power profile, may run the laptop but never charge it, or not start it at all.
CommonWorn charging port or DC jack
The plug feels loose, or charging returns only when it is angled or pushed in. Port repair
SometimesShort or charging-circuit fault in the laptop
The adapter protects itself and its light dims or goes out when connected. The fault is on the board, and a new charger would be wasted money. Chip-level repair

Safe checks you can do first

  1. Plug the charger into the wall only, without the laptop. A light that stays off means the adapter or cable.
  2. Now connect the laptop. If the light dims or goes out, stop: the fault is in the laptop and repeated attempts can make it worse.
  3. Bend the cable gently near both ends while watching the light. Flickering means a broken wire inside.
  4. Read the label on the brick: the wattage (W) and voltage must match the laptop's original charger.
  5. Try a known-good charger of the same type and wattage if you can borrow one.

What not to do

  • Do not keep using a charger with a cut, taped or melted cable; it can overheat.
  • Do not buy a cheaper, lower-wattage charger to save money; it strains the charging circuit and may never charge the battery.
  • Do not keep plugging in a laptop that makes the charger light go out.

Questions people ask

Before you decide

Can a laptop charger be repaired?

Most adapter bricks are sealed and are replaced rather than repaired, and a damaged cable is not worth patching because of the heat risk. What matters more is confirming the charger really is the fault before buying one.

Is a compatible (non-original) charger safe?

A good compatible charger with the correct voltage, wattage and plug from a reputable maker is fine, and it is what most people use. Very cheap unbranded adapters are the ones to avoid. Original vs compatible parts.

Can I use a phone charger on a USB-C laptop?

It may charge very slowly or not at all, because most phone chargers deliver far less power than a laptop needs. Use a USB-C charger rated at the wattage of the original.
