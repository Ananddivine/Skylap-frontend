Quick answer

Three situations, three answers. Laptop dead, drive healthy: we read the drive in another machine and copy your files, or repair the laptop with the drive untouched. Deleted or formatted, drive healthy: software recovery, high success. Drive failing (clicking, not detected, bad sectors): we image what we can here and tell you honestly when the job needs a clean-room lab, which we can refer you to. Nothing is charged for a recovery that yields nothing.

Symptoms this covers

  • Laptop will not start and the files were never backed up
  • "Hard disk got crashed", clicking or grinding sounds
  • "No boot device found" or the drive missing from BIOS
  • Bad sectors: files that will not open, freezes on access
  • Accidentally deleted or formatted
  • SSD suddenly not detected

Likely causes, in plain words

CommonLaptop fault, drive fine
Board or power failure; the drive is untouched. Recovery is straightforward. Recovering data from a dead laptop
CommonLogical loss: deleted, formatted, corrupted file system
Data is still on the platters or cells; recovery software reads it back if the drive has not been written to since.
SometimesBad sectors and slow failure
A hard disk wearing out. We image the drive sector by sector, skipping bad areas, then recover from the image.
SometimesMechanical failure (heads, motor)
Clicking means the heads. This needs a clean room and donor parts: a lab job, not a workshop job. We say so.
RareSSD controller or NAND failure
A dead SSD is harder than a dead hard disk; chip-level recovery exists but is expensive and not always possible.

How we diagnose it

  1. We ask what happened and stop any further use of the drive.
  2. We check whether the drive or the laptop is the problem.
  3. For a failing drive we image first, recover second; we never run recovery on the original.
  4. We tell you what is recoverable, what it costs, and when a lab is the honest route, before anything is charged.

Repair options

Recovery from a dead laptop

Drive removed, read in a healthy machine, files copied to your new drive or external disk.

Logical recovery

Deleted, formatted or corrupted file systems on a healthy drive.

Imaging a failing drive

Sector-by-sector imaging of a drive with bad sectors, then recovery from the image.

Lab referral

Mechanical hard disk failures and dead SSD controllers go to a clean-room lab. We prepare the drive and tell you what to expect.

What it costs

Recovery is quoted after we know which of the situations above you are in; the difference between them is large. No data, no charge for the recovery itself.

What decides the price

  • Logical vs failing vs mechanical vs SSD controller failure
  • Drive capacity and how much must be recovered
  • Encryption (BitLocker) and whether you have the key
  • Urgency
  • Whether a clean-room lab is required (lab quote, not ours)

We quote after a free diagnosis of your specific model, in writing, before any work starts. There is no charge if you decide not to go ahead.

Questions people ask

Before you decide

My laptop is dead. Can you still get my files off it?

In most cases, yes. A dead board does not affect the drive. We read the drive directly and copy your files, or repair the laptop with the drive left untouched.

Is my data safe when I hand my laptop in for repair?

Your drive stays in the laptop unless the repair needs it out, and we do not access your files. If the drive must come out, you see it happen and it is labelled with your name. Tell us if you would like a backup taken first.

The disk is clicking. What should I do?

Switch off and stop trying to boot it. Every attempt with failing heads scratches the platters. Bring it in; we assess whether imaging is possible here or a lab is needed.

Do you charge if nothing can be recovered?

No charge for a recovery that yields nothing. If a lab is needed, the lab quotes separately and you decide.

Not sure which one you have?

Describe it in a sentence or send a photo. We tell you what it is before you spend anything.
